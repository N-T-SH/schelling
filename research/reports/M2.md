# M2 Harness and simple baselines: report

**Result:** the evaluation harness and baselines B0 to B3 are built and run on both sources. On Twin-2K-500 the item-mean baseline B1 is hard to beat (A09): the gradient-boosted B3 improves its NLL by a hair and the demographic-cell B2 is worse. On Psych-101 the item-mean baseline is worse than uniform because the item ids and key-letter options do not transfer across participants, which needs your decision (finding 1). Every number below is tagged to a `metrics.json` and checked by `scripts/audit_report.py`.

All runs: validation partition only, seed 0, 20 population seeds, code revision 6ef570f with a clean tree, baselines fitted on the train partition of the same split. The test partition was never read.

## What was built

- `src/schelling/eval/`: `metrics.py`, `population.py`, `retest.py`, `robustness.py`, `efficiency.py`, `effects.py`, `provenance.py`, `schema.py`, `report.py`. Every `metrics.json` carries `git_sha`, `dirty`, `config_hash`, `split_manifest_hash`, `seed` and `created_at`, and validates against `schemas/metrics.json`.
- `src/schelling/baselines/`: shared `Baseline` protocol and B0 to B3.
- `scripts/audit_report.py`: fails on a tagged number that differs from its source, and on any untagged decimal or percentage in the prose.
- CLI: `schelling eval --model b0|b1|b2|b3 --split S --partition val`. It refuses `--partition test` without the human's environment variable (tested).
- Loader: split seed and contamination removals now come from the committed `splits/<split>.json`, so an evaluation cannot drift from the reviewed split.

## Assumptions

- A08 verified by simulation (`tests/unit/test_population.py`): sampling one draw per person from the correct per-person probabilities gives an SD ratio near 1, and sharpening the same probabilities lowers it.
- A09 partly true: on Twin-2K-500 B1 is beaten only marginally by B3 and not at all by B2 (table above). Psych-101 is a different story (finding 1).
- A14 verified for the count and the ceiling: every `retest` validation row has a wave-1 partner, and the ceiling is the first column of the retest table.
- A15 verified (LightGBM needed `brew install libomp` on this Mac).

## Findings that need your decision

1. **B1 is worse than uniform on Psych-101, and the cause is the data, not the baseline.** I checked the held-out rows: most Psych-101 trials share one item id per experiment ("You press"), because the item is the text before the choice, and the choices are key letters whose meaning differs by participant. For example, in one experiment the train person pressed only the first and last of four keys while the held-out person pressed only the middle two, so the train marginal puts almost no mass on what the held-out person does. On items with a hundred or more train rows B1 averages far above the uniform NLL, and for experiments absent from train it falls back to uniform (rows counted in `individual.unseen_item_rows`). This is the M1 finding about the unit of prediction showing up in the numbers. "Beat B1 on every source" is therefore not a meaningful bar on Psych-101 with the current adapter. Options: (a) keep the adapter, report Psych-101 baseline results as uninformative, and settle the unit of prediction in campaign C04 before M4; (b) restrict Psych-101 to experiments whose options are semantic, which needs an adapter change and so must happen before the manifests and splits are sealed at sign-off; (c) accept as is. I recommend (a), with Psych-101 excluded from the M4 acceptance bar until C04 is done.
2. **Treatment effects and regression sign agreement are not run.** `effects.py` is implemented and tested, but there is no pre-registered table pairing between-subject conditions (for example the anchoring high and low blocks) with expected effects. I can draft that table for your review.
3. **Population metrics on Psych-101 rest on very few items** (see `population.psych101.n_items` in each `metrics.json`) because of the 1 per cent sample and the collapsed item ids. Treat them as a smoke test, not a result.

## Deviations and choices to review

- Metric details I fixed (the evaluator is sealed once you sign off): top-label ECE with equal-mass bins where identical confidences share a bin (otherwise a uniform predictor gets spurious error from arbitrary tie order); Wasserstein over option index with unit spacing, so it is only reported for score and yes/no items; SD ratio only for ordered items; mode collapse means the synthetic modal share exceeds the human one by more than a fifth.
- B1 uses a pseudo-count of half per option (`alpha`); B2 cells are sex by age band by education with backoff below 20 people; B3 is one LightGBM model per question type and option count, 100 rounds, with item id as a categorical feature. These were set before looking at validation results and not tuned.
- Retest: baselines are fitted on wave-1 answers of persons we do not score. Agreement means the chance that one draw matches the wave-4 answer; the human ceiling uses the person's own wave-1 answer.
- Paraphrase invariance and the memorisation probe are not run (they need paraphrases, M5); the list is in each `metrics.json` under `not_run`.
- Fixes to my own earlier work: a test listing `eval` as a stub was updated in its own commit; a hand-computed Wasserstein expectation in my new test was wrong and corrected (verified by hand); the dirty flag ignores run outputs under `reports/`.
- Not checked: CI on GitHub for the M2 commits had not finished when this report was written.
